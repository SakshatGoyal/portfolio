# Hitachi Energy + Cisco case-study scorecard

Scoring method: each criterion is scored from 0–5. A dimension score is the sum of its seven criteria divided by 35, expressed as a percentage. Release threshold: every dimension must score at least 95% independently. A 5 means the work is portfolio-ready with no material weakness; a 4 means strong but visibly improvable; 3 or below requires revision.

## 1. Alignment with existing case-study writing quality

1. **Immediate project framing** — The headline and opening establish the business, user, product, designer’s role, and central design challenge without throat-clearing.
2. **Clear narrative progression** — Context, evidence, decisions, iterations, outcomes, and reflection form a coherent story rather than a chronological activity log.
3. **Specific senior-level ownership** — The writing names consequential judgments, tradeoffs, cross-functional influence, and the designer’s actual contribution without inflating claims.
4. **Evidence-led design rationale** — Research, artifacts, and product constraints explain why decisions changed; conclusions are not presented as unsupported taste.
5. **Human, natural voice** — Copy is direct, confident, and readable, avoiding jargon, generic portfolio language, strained cleverness, defensiveness, and legalistic caveats.
6. **Editorial economy and rhythm** — Paragraphs, headings, emphasis, and captions vary pace, prevent repetition, and sustain attention at the density of the four existing studies.
7. **Credible outcome and reflection** — Impact is precise and proportionate to available evidence, while lessons reveal mature product-design judgment.

## 2. Alignment with existing visual and aesthetic style

1. **Shared visual language** — Typography, color behavior, spacing, borders, captions, navigation, and media treatment clearly belong to the same portfolio system.
2. **Case-specific identity** — Each study gains a distinct but restrained palette and visual motif without appearing to be a separate website.
3. **Editorial visual rhythm** — Reading-column passages alternate with full-width, paired, or asymmetric media in a deliberate cadence comparable to the existing studies.
4. **Asset selection and sequencing** — Every image or video advances the argument, appears near the decision it supports, and avoids redundant or weak screens.
5. **Caption quality and completeness** — Every visual, including hero media, carousels, diagrams, and videos, has a useful caption that adds context rather than restating the image.
6. **Media presentation quality** — Cropping, scale, containment, poster frames, playback behavior, resolution, and background treatment make source assets feel polished and intentional.
7. **Responsive and accessible continuity** — Desktop and mobile preserve hierarchy, legibility, keyboard behavior, meaningful alt text, and comfortable pacing.

## 3. Overall craft and reviewer impression

1. **Senior-role signal** — Within the first screen and first major decision, a hiring reviewer can see strategic scope, product judgment, and collaboration maturity.
2. **Memorable product thesis** — Each project has a distinct, repeatable idea: Hitachi as partner–account-team commerce collaboration; Cisco as a core insight product extended by contributed opportunity intelligence.
3. **End-to-end coherence** — Story, visuals, captions, metadata, navigation, homepage cards, and case sequencing reinforce the same positioning with no loose ends.
4. **Interaction polish** — Video, carousel, lightbox, links, hover/focus states, and navigation feel deliberate and free of distracting failure states.
5. **Technical finish** — Pages build cleanly, load without broken assets or console errors, avoid layout shifts, and keep media performant.
6. **Trust and authenticity** — Claims, dates, names, data, confidentiality handling, and captions feel accurate, candid, and appropriately bounded.
7. **Final impression** — The pages feel authored, distinctive, and complete enough to strengthen a senior product-design application rather than merely add project volume.
