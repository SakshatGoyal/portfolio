## Insight 01

### Structured frameworks and scenario planning create clarity amid uncertainty, yet reliance on informal networks highlights that real clarity often comes from unexpected, unstructured interactions.

**THEME - 01:**
Leaders leverage structured decision-making frameworks and mental models as tools to reduce ambiguity, and bring clarity to challenging scenarios.

> "All my decision-making majority is based on the programs that I've established--regulatory management, audit management, compliance... consider external factors before making any strategic decisions."
>
> -- Dennis

> "[We] start out with like insights... come up with like strategic imperatives... then sub strategies... then tactics... and scenario plan."
>
> -- Karen

**THEME - 02:**
Executives commonly tap into informal networks, (peers conversations, competitors, industry insiders) to glean insights and challenge assumptions.

> "We use everything from industry groups to AI to identify what [risk] looks like... The ambiguity--comes the experience considering all the variables at hand, gaining as much additional information for clarity of that ambiguity."
>
> -- Dennis

> "A lot of it is just talking to other people in the industry... tapping into people's personal networks and talking to friends... to get a sense of what are the things they're seeing and dealing with."
>
> -- Akhil

## Insight 02

### Leaders acknowledge AI Accelerates Strategic Insight Generation, Yet Sensitive Data Constraints Limit Depth.

**THEME - 03:**
Respondents show openness to using GenAI for certain supportive, non-critical tasks, particularly around research, synthesis, and initial ideation.

> "I am not skeptical at all that a generative model would be able to produce blind spots for us, but I am fully skeptical that it would be able to de-noise that list of blind spots."
>
> -- Tim

> "I've become completely addicted to ChatGPT. It's like, I call it my summer intern."
>
> -- Karen

**THEME - 04:**
Leaders express caution regarding proprietary or sensitive internal information being shared with generative AI platforms.

> "I don't really want to see the person saying, hey, ChatGPT, here's the roadmap that I came up with. You now generate the blind spots... you're giving it an artifact that is proprietary to Squarespace, and you're saying analyze this artifact--that's a little bit less okay."
>
> -- Tim

> "My first reaction would be hesitation... I want to make sure that if we are using an application like that and inputting or imputing our own proprietary data to any degree, that there's no compliance concerns internally."
>
> -- Akhil

## Insight 03

### Executives Emphasize Human Judgment as Irreplaceable, Yet Increasingly Delegate Critical Early-stage Thinking to AI

**THEME - 05:**
Executives uniformly emphasize that despite GenAI's analytical capabilities, strategic judgment and contextual decision-making ultimately remain firmly in the human domain.

> "[AI is good] from a research and diligence perspective... but the practicality of understanding the realities of the business and capabilities and the way resources are deployed, and strategy of the company at a single point in time, like that's impossible to layer, and that is just like knowledge and experience, and so can't ever fully replace it."
>
> -- Akhil

> "AI gives output; judgment is still required... We must challenge the outputs of AI. So I think that's a basic requirement of anyone in my position."
>
> -- Dennis

**THEME - 06:**
Leaders clearly define acceptable and unacceptable uses for GenAI, generally favoring tasks such as research, first drafts, and creative output, while categorically excluding mission-critical decisions or strategic framing.

> "There's probably three different ways we can think about how tools get employed... We don't do that [strategic raw data use] very often... The second area is as a creative partner... that's an area we use generative AI quite a bit."
>
> -- Tim

> "We're doing some experimentation... having the right guardrails internally from like a compliance legal perspective to make sure we're surfacing and using that information in the right way."
>
> -- Akhil

## Insight 04

### While executives seek AI automation for analytics, they maintain human roles for stakeholder coordination, showing continued reliance on personal interaction.

**THEME - 07:**
Respondents envisioned an AI-enabled role specifically tasked with data synthesis, insight generation, summarization, and routine analytical tasks.

> "We would have an AI that I'm comfortable putting in all of my policies, procedures, risk management programs... help us identify where our risks are from audit and regulatory perspective. That type of tool if it exists would be great."
>
> -- Dennis

> "Running data analysis and summarizing data to come up with key takeaways, visuals, slides, memos... we spend a lot of time doing analysis ourselves... [a role that] 10x our output by using our people to layer in situational understanding on top of synthesized data."
>
> -- Akhil

**THEME - 08:**
A frequent idea was to create a role explicitly responsible for managing operational execution, like detailed stakeholder coordination and cross-functional alignment.

> "Project management type work is something I'd delegate--how to organize people, organize a project, get decisions made, drive elements of work forward... managing cross-functional teams."
>
> -- Akhil

> "[Managing] overlap, making sure everyone is coordinated... there's value that would come out of that meeting if I were in it... someone who sees interdependencies, makes connections, and shares them."
>
> -- Karen
