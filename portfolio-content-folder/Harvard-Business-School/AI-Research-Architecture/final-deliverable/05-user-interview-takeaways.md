## Takeaway 01: Prioritize Strategic Courage over Strategic Certainty

Data gives us confidence. Frameworks give us structure. But real **strategy often requires action before the data is conclusive.** We argue that most tools, even AI, tend to reinforce the executive preference for certainty. What's missing is an intelligent prompt toward courage.

This is not about being reckless. It's about **recognizing 'when' the pursuit of more validation is simply masking a reluctance to commit and then designing tools that help break that loop.**

### Examples

**EXAMPLE - 01:**
Tim described the Super Bowl homepage campaign: all energy went into a short-term spike with uncertain value.

He had to anchor the team in long-term strategic relevance amid the urgency. He did the emotional labor of contextualizing against short-term pressure

What if an AI could've somehow reinforced that in a different situation?

**EXAMPLE - 02:**
Akhil talked about short-term decisions that undermined long-term scalability.

One example: (while talking about an acquisition) customizing early deals that later broke the model.

An AI prompting, "Does this decision reinforce your 5-year architecture, or just solve this quarter's fire?" could help put courage in tension with short-term incentive structures

## Takeaway 02: Move from "Lessons Learned" to "Lessons Unlearned"

Executives and organizations are exceptionally good at codifying success through retrospectives, personal playbooks, and frameworks. But **they may struggle with letting go of the assumptions, habits, and past logics that no longer serve them in new contexts.**

This takeaway invites us to flip the script: **strategic maturity isn't just about learning more, it's about knowing when to unlearn.** AI can play a critical role here, not by adding insight, but by gently challenging institutional muscle memory.

### Example

Dennis spoke about the overwhelming volume of available information and the difficulty of locating **"golden sources"**, but I thought he didn't question whether some of those sources are simply outdated.

**A prompt to unlearn would have reframed his challenge not just as a filtering issue, but as a relevance issue**
