# 14. What exactly does “consistency” measure, and how do the 50%, 65%, 84%, 85%, 88%, and 95% figures relate?

**Category:** Missing information or evidence  
**Caliber markers:** Consequence, provenance, defensibility

**Question:** Please reconcile the model-evaluation claims across the folder: approximately 50% GPT-4 variation, 35% o3-mini-high variation and 65% derived alignment, GPT-4.5 at roughly 84% in iteration five, o3-high at roughly 85%, GPT-4.5 at 88% in iteration seven, and O1 Pro at 95%. Were these the same task, dataset, denominator, scoring method, and definition of agreement?

**Reason for investigation:** The project makes model selection part of the design story, which is valuable. The artifacts, however, appear to combine several measures: repeat-run output variation, derived alignment, identical-score concentration, qualitative labels such as “intuitive” or “well-reasoned,” and percentages in an eight-iteration table. The reconstructed mini case explicitly says original run-level data and some sample sizes were not retained. Its final recommendation selects o3-mini-high as the measured default even though another artifact visually places other models at higher consistency.

A public-product PM would not know which number supports the investment claim. An enterprise AI leader would require metric definitions, comparable conditions, confidence intervals, model versions, and a reason higher consistency did not automatically win. At Staff level, measurement ambiguity in a project about research reliability is load-bearing.

**Learning objective and decision relevance:** I want a single evidence table defining each test, dataset, sample, rubric, evaluator, model version, prompt version, and result. A strong answer would explicitly retract comparisons that are not valid and explain why interpretive depth justified a lower consistency score where appropriate. Honest reconciliation would strengthen the work. Treating all percentages as interchangeable improvement evidence would substantially weaken trust in the pipeline.

## Answer

They don't reconcile into one table, and the numbers come from two different measurement families, run at different times on different tasks with different denominators, and the original run-level data was not retained. Anyone presenting them as one benchmark — including a careless reading of my own portfolio — is wrong. Here is the actual anatomy.

The definition first, because it's the stable part. Consistency meant two-run overlap: take a batch of modules, attach the prompt and its reference documents — the definitions of ideal tagging, the tiebreaker rules — run the tagging, then run it again fresh on identical input, and measure how much of the output agrees with itself. It's a repeatability claim about the prompt-plus-model system, nothing more: not accuracy, not truth — a model can be consistently wrong — but a necessary floor, because a tagger that disagrees with itself at 15 modules cannot be trusted at a thousand. Interpretive depth was the paired qualitative judgment: is the rationale literal, contextual, well-reasoned, intuitive, or imaginative.

Family one — the eight blind prompt iterations. Same goal held fixed; prompts, definitions, examples, and reference documents evolved between iterations; consistency measured as two-run overlap, depth judged on the five-degree scale. That family produced the trajectory numbers: GPT-4o wandering ~36–50% and never escaping "imaginative" before being discontinued; o3-high climbing ~65% to ~85% but plateauing at "well-reasoned," never intuitive; o1-pro at ~95% but "literal" — a benchmark of consistency, useless for depth — discontinued after two iterations; GPT-4.5 the volatile one, ~50% up to ~84% at iteration five while staying intuitive, ~88% at iteration seven after further hardening, then ~72% at iteration eight. That last drop is the most instructive number in the set: a reference-document change that I expected to help degraded consistency, which is what taught me the prompt and its references behave as one designed system, not independently tunable parts.

Family two — the tagging-variation record, reconstructed later in the mini case study, which says so on its face. There, GPT-4 baseline showed roughly 50% variation across repeated runs and o3-mini-high roughly 35%; "65% alignment" is nothing more exotic than 100 minus 35. Different task (the categorical rubric tagging), different metric direction (variation observed, alignment derived), different moment. And floating near both families is a third indicator that is not consistency at all: score concentration — the 17-criterion rubric's collapse where 30 of 30 modules received the identical 153, hardening to 17 of 30 — which measures whether an instrument differentiates within a batch, not whether it repeats across runs. The 43-point improvement there is a differentiation number and must never be averaged with anything above.

So why did o3-mini-high become the production default when the table shows other models scoring higher? Because the families answer different questions and the decision consumed both plus two unmeasured axes. o1-pro's 95% bought literalness — maximal consistency at minimal depth, exactly the false-precision trap. GPT-4.5's 84–88% was iteration-fragile and expensive at volume, so it became the stress-tester, not the tagger. Full o3 had the best qualitative rubric fidelity and became the escalation tier. o3-mini-high's 65% was the best measured repeat-run result in its family at a cost and throughput that survived hundreds of modules — a satisficing pick under a depth floor, not a leaderboard win. What I'd retract under oath: any cross-family comparison, all confidence intervals (there are none; single evaluator, small batches, tildes throughout), and the sample sizes behind the variation figures, which were not retained. The percentages are directional internal evidence from a project deciding which tool to trust, and I defend them only in that register. In a project about evaluating plausible-sounding AI output, letting my own numbers sound more precise than they are would be the exact failure I spent four months fighting — which is why the mini case carries its scope note in the document itself.
