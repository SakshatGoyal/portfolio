# 16. What durable product, organizational capability, or decision outlived the research package?

**Category:** Missing information or evidence  
**Caliber markers:** Consequence, force multiplication, corroboration

**Question:** After the final research architecture was delivered, what did HBS or the product team build, stop, fund, test, or do differently? Which schemas, principles, prompts, taxonomies, or evaluation practices continued without the designer, and what evidence shows that other teams became more effective because of the work?

**Reason for investigation:** The project contains unusually extensive artifacts and several potentially reusable systems: structured insight modules, evidence labeling, strategy classification, inquiry routing, situational axes, archetypes, tradeoff-based principles, scope matrices, and tiered model selection. The portfolio says these provided a foundation for product teams, but the folder does not show an adopted MVP brief, a product concept, downstream research, a maintained repository, team reuse, a leadership decision, or feedback from the people who received the work.

A public-product PM would ask whether the research reduced time to a better product decision or simply expanded the landscape. An enterprise leader would ask who owns maintenance, how the framework adapts as models and sources change, and whether its complexity creates a new dependency on the designer. Force multiplication—not artifact volume—is the Staff-level gate.

**Learning objective and decision relevance:** A strong answer would name the downstream decision, date, owner, reused artifacts, product or research change, and corroborating partner. It could also show that the work responsibly stopped an unjustified product direction; prevention is a real outcome if the causal chain is clear. If the architecture ended as a polished handoff with no durable adoption, I would understand it as ambitious and thoughtful research design, but not yet evidence of Staff-level organizational multiplication.

## Answer

I'll inventory this in three tiers — proven, probable, and unproven — because the force-multiplication question deserves an answer that doesn't launder hope into evidence.

Proven, with artifacts: the reusable machinery exists and outlived the engagement in my own practice. The insight-module schema with claim-level evidence tags, the situational-axes instrument, the principle/counter-principle format, the calibration protocol (blind iterations, consistency-times-depth, tiered model routing), and the 21-test routing method are all documented, portable systems — and the persona-emulation line (PESS) that spun out of a week-16 side thread continued as its own program after the core project wound down, which makes it the one demonstrable case of this work seeding a successor program rather than just a deliverable. The engineer's observation — "what you've built is a pipeline; try running it with Gemini or Claude" — was itself a reuse claim from an adjacent discipline: he recognized the artifact as infrastructure, not a report. I quote him because an engineer calling a designer's research system a pipeline is corroborable texture, and because his suggestion named the portability I hadn't tested.

Probable, but resting on my testimony rather than documents: the framing survived at the institute in conversation — the archetype vocabulary and the theme names functioned as shared language with the Head of AI and the PM by the end of the engagement, and the paired-principle format was used to fine-tune the voice of an executive-facing AI system. That last is the closest thing to a product consequence in the record, and I state it at its real size: calibration material for tone, not an adopted interaction architecture.

Unproven: organizational adoption at HBS without me. The archive contains no adopted MVP brief, no downstream research program citing the modules, no maintained repository handed to a team, no leadership decision memo, no feedback artifact from the people who received the work. The causal chain ends at delivery. I did not instrument the afterlife of the deliverable — no follow-up interviews, no six-month check-in on which scoping approach was taken — and that omission is mine. The uncomfortable structural truth underneath it: a system this bespoke has a dependency problem. The pipeline ran on my prompts, my reference documents, my evaluation practice; the deliverable was designed to be legible without me, but the machinery was not operable without me, and durable capability means the machinery, not the PDFs. If HBS never reran the pipeline, the most likely reason is the documented one — I built an instrument and didn't finish building the instrumentalist.

Could I claim prevention — that the work responsibly stopped an unjustified direction? The material supports a modest version: the research demonstrated that "an AI strategist for executives," taken whole, is not a product but a category error — the corpus showed executive strategy fracturing into structurally different situations needing different postures, which is an argument against building the naive general assistant the Zareen fiction imagined in February. If the institute never built that naive product, the research is plausibly part of why. But "plausibly part of why" is not a causal chain, and no one recorded a decision not to build something, because organizations don't write memos about roads not taken. That absence is not an outcome.

So the final accounting, in the register this whole project was built in: [Empirical] — the methods, schemas, and instruments persist and one successor program demonstrably grew from them. [Inferred] — the framing shaped how the institute's AI team talked about executive users, attested by me, corroborable by two named-by-role people. [Speculative] — any claim that HBS builds, funds, or stops anything differently because this architecture existed. A Staff-level story wants tier three; the evidence supports tiers one and two; and the most durable thing I can prove this project produced is the discipline of labeling which tier a claim lives in — which is, not accidentally, the sentence you are reading demonstrating itself.
