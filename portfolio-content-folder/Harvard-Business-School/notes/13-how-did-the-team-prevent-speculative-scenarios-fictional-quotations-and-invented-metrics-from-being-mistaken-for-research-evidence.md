# 13. How did the team prevent speculative scenarios, fictional quotations, and invented metrics from being mistaken for research evidence?

**Category:** The work is understandable but concerning  
**Caliber markers:** Provenance, narrative command, defensibility

**Question:** What labeling and review rules separated empirical findings, inferred implications, illustrative futures, and fully fictional success measures across the sixteen scenario sheets? Why are some sections marked “[speculated]” while others contain imagined company outcomes, quotations, and precise percentages without the same visible warning?

**Reason for investigation:** Speculative scenarios can be excellent design tools, especially when a team needs to make an abstract future concrete. The problem is evidentiary blending. The scenario documents include plausible organizations, detailed operating changes, invented dialogue, and figures such as onboarding reductions, emotional-ownership increases, MRR change, and renewal improvement. Some scenarios explicitly admit insufficient knowledge; others use research-sounding language before moving into imagined outcomes. The boundary is not consistently legible.

A public-product PM might mistakenly prioritize an opportunity because an illustrative outcome looks measured. An enterprise research leader would worry about unsupported company claims, citation quality, reputational risk, and synthetic evidence feeding later models or decisions. At Staff level, the designer should make uncertainty visible in the artifact, not require an oral disclaimer.

**Learning objective and decision relevance:** A strong answer would define the evidence taxonomy, show how every claim links to a source or an explicit speculative label, explain the purpose of invented metrics, and identify review partners. It would also describe how the scenarios were used without becoming “facts” in later work. If the unlabeled blending was a production artifact, correcting it is essential. If the team intentionally used speculative specificity to provoke discussion, the facilitation and containment mechanism must be clear.

## Answer

Through three containment mechanisms built into the artifacts — and imperfectly, with at least one production failure I'll show you myself, because the failure defines where the containment actually stood.

The mechanisms, bottom to top. First, provenance capture at extraction: every insight module carried [Empirical], [Inferred], or [Speculative] tags at the claim level, applied at the moment the model first touched a source — Module 24's insight statement is tagged [Empirical] while its own broader-implications block, in the same module, is tagged [Inferred]. Provenance was in the data structure, not retrofitted. Second, anti-speculation rules in the synthesis prompts, verbatim: "Do not hallucinate or extrapolate beyond the .txt source... Inferring is allowed, but make sure you are not assigning artificial or unproven problems to the archetypes." The people-problem statements in the sixteen cells had to trace to modules, and the traceback I ran confirms they do. Third, section-level fencing in the shipped cards: the success-criteria sections that outran the evidence carry a bolded [speculated] flag — eleven such flags appear across the sixteen cells — and one cell (Legacy Navigators × Underestimated Complexity) states outright that its criteria are "currently hard to define without further exploration." The deliverable states the limit of its own knowledge in print.

Why the speculative layer existed at all: there was no product, no division, no chosen audience — the most abstract brief imaginable — and the sixteen problem statements, drawn from research, were too abstract for a mixed audience to hold. The scenarios were built as comprehension tools and as North-star generators: walk through what the problem looks like inside an imagined company, then describe the world where it's solved, and the solved-world description yields candidate success measures. The invented specifics — the imagined quality scorecards, the post-mortem dialogue, the fictional collaborations — exist to make "how might we know" concrete. Every imagined outcome sits inside a section whose header is a question ("How might we know if we've solved this problem?") under a [speculated] flag, and some cells even declare their own construction in first person: "I think a Global Consumer Goods business could be a strong enough illustrative case to craft a meaningful speculative scenario."

Now the failure, precisely located. The fence was section-level, not claim-level, and the question is right that the boundary is not consistently legible. Inside speculated sections, invented dialogue and precise-sounding figures appear without per-line markers, and research-register language ("research from executive interviews reveals") opens at least one cell whose evidence is thinner than the sentence sounds — four interviews carrying a sentence written for a corpus. Worse, and I flag it as the clearest production defect: at least one shipped cell contains a garbled sentence — "from eBay receiving data is eBay observing what defines value proposition" — that is recognizably unedited model output which survived my review into the deliverable. That sentence is harmless in content and damning in implication: it proves the final editorial pass over the speculative sections was thinner than the pass over the evidence sections. My review attention followed my own risk model — I policed the problem statements hardest because they carried the evidentiary claims — and the speculated sections, pre-fenced as speculation, got proportionally less scrutiny. Defensible triage; visible cost.

The system worked as follows: empirical claims were containment-checked at three layers and trace to source papers; speculative material was quarantined by section and flag rather than line by line; and the quarantine relied on readers respecting the fence. A hurried reader who lifts a number out of a [speculated] section gets fiction with confident formatting, and the artifact does not stop them at the sentence level. The correction I'd make in any future version costs little: a visual register shift — different typography for speculative prose, not just a flag above it — so the boundary survives skimming. The mechanisms were real, the intent was legible, the enforcement was as deep as one reviewer's attention, and I've shown you where it ran out.
