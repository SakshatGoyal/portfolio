# 3. How was the source corpus selected, and what evidence shows it represented the strategic terrain rather than the search strategy?

**Category:** Missing information or evidence  
**Caliber markers:** Provenance, defensibility, consequence

**Question:** How many papers entered and survived the corpus, which databases and search terms were used, what inclusion and exclusion criteria applied, how were academic and industry sources weighted, and how did you test whether the selected industries and publications distorted the resulting archetypes?

**Reason for investigation:** The pipeline imagery describes three research questions, six strategy types, industry-specific prompts, hundreds of papers, and 992 structured insight modules across industries such as aerospace, automotive, finance, medicine, entertainment, retail, and software. That scale looks rigorous, but scale can amplify selection bias. The folder does not contain a searchable bibliography, rejection log, source-quality rubric, coverage analysis, or evidence that the corpus reached saturation. Some scenario claims cite recognizable companies or numerical findings without visible citations, while other passages are explicitly speculative.

A public-product PM would not accept corpus size as a proxy for relevance; they would ask whether the material represented the eventual user and decision. An enterprise research leader would expect source lineage, versioning, licensing awareness, quality tiers, and the ability to reproduce every downstream claim. At Staff level, the designer should be able to explain how the evidence architecture prevented the research question from manufacturing its own answer.

**Learning objective and decision relevance:** A strong answer would show the corpus manifest, selection criteria, source distribution, exclusion reasons, saturation signal, and at least one conclusion that changed when new evidence entered. I also want to know which claims can be traced to specific sources. If those records exist, the 992 modules become a defensible evidence base. If they do not, the architecture may be visually systematic while remaining epistemically fragile.

## Answer

The corpus represented the search strategy — every corpus does — and the design question was whether the search strategy was built to defeat my biases or to flatter them. I can show the defeat mechanism; I cannot show saturation, and I'll be precise about both.

Five days in, on February 25, I wrote down the thing most likely to invalidate the project: I'm a UX designer, my professional exposure is companies with digital products, and left to instinct I would build an executive-decision corpus out of tech firms. So the sourcing rule was designed as bias-control before it was a collection plan. The structure was a grid: six strategy classifications crossed with industries, where for each classification I forced the model's candidate industry lists through a pruning rule — keep only the five most contrasting industries — and vetoed tech-leaning defaults, forcing in agriculture, supply chain, pharma, energy, private equity. A hierarchy dimension (C-level, executive, upper management) shaped whose decisions the sources had to describe. Collection targets were set per decision scenario, roughly five to seven sources each.

What actually entered the pipeline, by the traceback I ran later: 238 PDFs plus 52 AI deep-research documents, across 30 populated grid cells. The published figure says 237 papers; the archive counts 238 files. I have not reconciled the off-by-one and I say so rather than rounding it away. Two structural gaps are also on record: the entire C6 row of the grid was never populated — a slot that existed in the design and got zero files — and cell C1-I7 has insight modules but no corresponding paper folder, meaning two modules' sources are filed somewhere I can no longer point to.

Now the uncomfortable parts, in order of severity. First, 52 of the ~290 source documents were themselves AI-generated deep-research outputs. I subjected them to the same downstream extraction, stress-testing, and evidence tagging as the PDFs, but a synthesized source cannot be stress-tested back to primary evidence the way a paper can, and a fully rigorous version of this corpus would have quarantined them in their own evidence tier. I treated the tags as the containment; that containment is real but thinner there. Second, there is no rejection log. I can show you the pruning rule and the vetoes in the chat record; I cannot produce the list of papers considered and excluded, because collection ran through search sessions that recorded keeps, not discards. Third, there is no saturation analysis. Collection stopped when the grid cells were populated to target, which is a coverage criterion, not an evidence criterion.

Did the selection distort the archetypes? I have one concrete data point that it constrained them, which I treat as the visible tip of the distortion: during the manual clustering read, I had to tell the model outright that "we don't have any Real-Time-Reliant related stories to evaluate" — an entire region of the situational-axis space was empty because the corpus never captured it. An archetype that might live there does not exist in my deliverable. I flagged that as a corpus boundary at the time rather than letting the model hallucinate the missing region, which is the containment working — but containment of a gap is not coverage of it.

The contrast rule demonstrably produced a corpus my instincts would not have (luxury retail, military crisis operations, clinical decision-making, QSR localization sit beside the SaaS papers). What I won't defend: any claim that ~290 documents "represent" executive strategy. They represent a disciplined, auditable slice, with its empty cells named.
