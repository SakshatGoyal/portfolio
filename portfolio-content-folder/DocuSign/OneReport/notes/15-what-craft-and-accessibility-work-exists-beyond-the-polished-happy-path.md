# 15. What craft and accessibility work exists beyond the polished happy path?

**Category:** The work appears not to have gone far enough  
**Caliber markers:** Craft at scan and zoom, defensibility

**Question:** How did you design and test loading, empty, stale, partial, conflicting, permission-denied, offline, error, and long-content states across Reports, AMA, and Analysis? How did the dense Sankey labels, small typography, color-coded flows, charts, sheets, and touch targets perform with accessibility settings and on real devices?

**Reason for investigation:** The visual concept passes the scan test: it has a coherent restrained style, polished hero compositions, and a distinctive flow visualization. At zoom, the evidence is thinner. Many labels and figures are extremely small, the Sankey paths are dense and cross repeatedly, the examples reuse placeholder-like values, and almost every state is successful. The scenario GIFs animate illustrations rather than demonstrating the actual product interaction.

At Staff level, craft must survive inspection and include states where the system is imperfect. A public-product design leader will inspect accessibility and interaction resilience. An enterprise leader will also care about degraded data, stale sources, and permission differences as routine—not edge—conditions.

**Learning objective and decision relevance:** I want real-device findings, accessibility decisions, state inventories, and at least one craft tradeoff made under technical constraint. If these were intentionally out of scope, the designer should state that the prototypes proved a narrow concept rather than a production-ready experience. Strong zoom-level evidence would materially improve the case; without it, I see capable concept craft but not proof of Staff-level executional depth.

## Answer

Very little, and the artifacts confess it if you zoom — so let me do the inspection on my own work before you do. Every quarter-over-quarter delta across the Sankey screens reads +12.87% or -12.87%; that's one placeholder token propagated through dozens of labels, a fidelity tell I left in deliberately rather than hand-randomizing values to fake realism (a debatable call — it kept the fiction internally consistent but reads as sloppiness without this explanation). The Marketing Executive Insights report shows "Total Spent: $275.12" — an absurd number nobody caught for weeks because everyone in the loop knew the data was fabricated. Sankey node labels truncate against the phone frame ("Commercial N…"), several value labels sit at sizes I'd estimate near 11pt, flow ribbons cross densely in the five-lens view, and the red/green delta encoding plus the color-coded flow families were never checked against any contrast standard or color-vision simulation. Touch targets on Sankey nodes are the flows themselves — thin, crossing, adjacent — and I never solved fat-finger disambiguation beyond assuming a tap-to-zoom that was discussed and not designed. State inventory: no loading, empty, stale, partial, permission-denied, offline, or error state was designed for any of the three surfaces. Not one screen of the concept shows the system being imperfect. The scenario GIFs animate illustrations of a person's day, not product interaction. Device exposure was my own phone plus prototypes in five directors' hands — informal, un-instrumented. Accessibility settings: untested entirely.

This was a scoping decision, made consciously, and I can name its terms. The prototypes existed to answer three questions for a director audience and a C-suite room: does the three-surface model map to real moments, is the analytical grammar learnable, and can AMA be cheap? Every craft hour went to the surfaces that carried those questions, on a concept with no committed engineering and a real chance — realized, as it happened — of never proceeding. Production-grade state and accessibility work on a possibly-dead concept is craft spent where no decision needs it. Degraded-data and permission-denied states were arguably load-bearing for the product's credibility and belonged in the concept. The later trust gap (Q10) and security deferral (Q11) strengthen that case. I'd concede it about the stale-data state specifically: for an executive analytics product, "this number is old" is arguably a hero state, and I never drew it.

Where craft-under-constraint genuinely shows, for balance: the label-card decision in the Sankey — white value cards floating over the flows rather than inline path labels — was chosen after inline labeling collapsed at mobile widths; it costs occlusion of the ribbons and buys legibility of the numbers, which for this audience was the correct trade. Keeping zero-nodes visible (Q6), the two-tap undo posture, and the bottom-thumb control bar all came from real in-hand iteration. One important constraint: the presented deck described the work as concept prototypes throughout — I never claimed production readiness to any audience, including here. The zoom-level verdict I'd write on my own review: strong concept craft, real interaction thinking, and a states-and-accessibility layer that is not thin but absent — deferred on purpose, documented nowhere at the time, and needed explicit acknowledgement.
